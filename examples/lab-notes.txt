The lab files — what each specimen is
====================================================================

All five files are invented for this guide.
No real log, account, customer, or order
appears in them. Copy them to a scratch
folder and work there (chapter 1 shows
how), so the originals survive experiments.


app.log — 56 lines
    A small server's log over three days,
    2026-10-17 to 2026-10-19. Every line is:
    date, time, level (INFO, WARN, ERROR,
    DEBUG), component (auth, http, cron,
    app, disk), then a message, often ending
    in key=value pairs (user=, ip=, reason=).
    Known answers: 10 ERROR lines (3, 5, and
    2 per day), 11 failed logins, and one
    address — 198.51.100.23 — that knocks
    more than anyone. External addresses
    are in the ranges reserved for
    documentation (198.51.100.0/24 and
    203.0.113.0/24); internal ones are
    192.168.1.x and 10.0.0.5.

passwd.txt — 12 lines
    Accounts in passwd format: name,
    password field (x), user id, group id,
    real name, home directory, shell —
    separated by colons. Five accounts have
    human-range uids (1000-1004); nobody
    sits at 65534, which is the guide's
    standing warning about numeric ranges.

orders.csv — 15 lines (header + 14 orders)
    Columns: order_id, date, customer,
    item, quantity, unit_price, status.
    Statuses are shipped (9), pending (3),
    and cancelled (2). Comma-clean by
    design: no quoted fields, no commas
    inside values. Shipped orders total
    248.66 in revenue — chapter 7 earns
    that number rather than trusting it.

app.conf — 10 lines
    Settings as "name = value", with
    comments starting #. db_host appears
    twice: once live, once commented out
    with an old address — recipe 4 in the
    guide is about not grabbing the wrong
    one.

messy.txt — 15 lines
    A shopping list with mixed capitals,
    leading and trailing spaces, one blank
    line, and duplicates that differ only
    by case or spaces. Eight real items
    hide in it. (This file's trailing
    spaces are deliberate — if your editor
    "helpfully" strips them on save, the
    mess is gone and the recipes have
    nothing to clean.)


Files the guide asks you to create
----------------------------------
    patterns.txt  two grep patterns, ch. 3
    fix.sed       three sed commands, ch. 5
    site/         a two-file tree for the
                  recursive search, ch. 3
Creating them is part of the lessons; the
chapters show their contents exactly.
